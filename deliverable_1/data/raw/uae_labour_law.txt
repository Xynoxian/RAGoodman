UAE LABOUR LAW
Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021
On the Regulation of Labour Relations

================================================================================

PART ONE: GENERAL PROVISIONS AND SCOPE

CHAPTER 1: Definitions and Scope of Application

Article 1:
This Law shall be known as the "Regulation of Labour Relations Law." It regulates the relationship between employers and workers in the private sector within the United Arab Emirates. The provisions herein aim to protect the rights of both parties, ensure fair working conditions, and promote a productive and stable labour market. This Law replaces Federal Law No. 8 of 1980 and its amendments.

Article 2:
For the purposes of this Law, the following terms shall have the meanings assigned to them:
(a) "Employer" means any natural or legal person who employs one or more workers in return for a wage.
(b) "Worker" or "Employee" means any natural person who works under the direction and supervision of an employer in return for a wage.
(c) "Wage" means the total remuneration paid to the worker in return for their services, including basic salary, allowances, bonuses, and any other benefits.
(d) "Probation Period" means a specified period at the beginning of employment during which the employer may assess the worker's suitability.
(e) "End of Service Gratuity" means the payment due to the worker upon termination of employment, calculated based on the duration of service.

Article 3:
This Law shall apply to all employers and workers in the private sector, free zones, and domestic workers, subject to specific provisions. The following categories are excluded from the scope of this Law:
(a) Members of the armed forces, police, and security agencies.
(b) Federal and local government employees governed by other legislation.
(c) Members of boards of directors of companies who do not hold an executive position.

CHAPTER 2: Employment Contracts

Article 4:
All employment contracts in the United Arab Emirates shall be of a fixed-term nature, with a maximum duration of three years, renewable by mutual agreement. Employment contracts previously concluded for an indefinite term shall be converted to fixed-term contracts within one year of the effective date of this Law. The employment contract shall be in writing and in duplicate, with each party retaining a copy.

Article 5:
The employment contract shall specify:
(a) The names and addresses of both parties.
(b) The date of commencement of employment.
(c) The nature and type of work to be performed.
(d) The workplace and the place of residence if the employer provides accommodation.
(e) The duration of the contract and conditions of renewal.
(f) The wage agreed upon, the method and date of payment.
(g) The daily and weekly working hours.
(h) The annual leave entitlement.
(i) The notice period for termination.
(j) Any other conditions agreed upon by both parties that do not conflict with this Law.

Article 6:
The probation period shall not exceed six months from the date of commencement of employment. During the probation period, either party may terminate the employment contract by giving fourteen days written notice. If the worker wishes to move to another employer within the State during the probation period, the new employer shall compensate the original employer for the recruitment costs incurred, unless otherwise agreed between the parties.

Article 7:
If the worker wishes to leave the State during the probation period, they may not return on a new work permit for a period of one year from the date of departure, unless they obtain the consent of the original employer. If the worker has completed the probation period and continues in employment, the probation period shall be counted as part of the total period of service.

CHAPTER 3: Working Hours, Leave, and Rest Periods

Article 8:
The maximum ordinary working hours shall be eight hours per day or forty-eight hours per week. The working hours may be reduced for arduous or hazardous work as determined by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation. The working hours during the Holy Month of Ramadan shall be reduced by two hours per day for all workers.

Article 9:
A worker shall not be required to work more than five consecutive hours without a break of not less than one hour for rest, meals, and prayer. Such break periods shall not be counted as part of the working hours. A worker shall be entitled to at least one rest day per week, which shall ordinarily be Friday. The employer may, with the approval of the Ministry, designate another day as the weekly rest day.

Article 10:
If the circumstances of work require the worker to work beyond the ordinary working hours, the additional hours shall be considered overtime. Overtime shall not exceed two hours per day, unless the work is necessary to prevent a serious loss or accident, or to address urgent technical issues. The worker shall be entitled to overtime pay calculated as the basic hourly wage plus an additional twenty-five percent. If the overtime is performed between 10:00 PM and 4:00 AM, the worker shall be entitled to the basic hourly wage plus an additional fifty percent. Work on rest days shall be compensated with another rest day or with overtime pay plus an additional fifty percent.

Article 11:
A worker who has completed one year of continuous service shall be entitled to annual leave as follows:
(a) Thirty calendar days per year for workers who have completed one year of service.
(b) Two calendar days per month for workers who have completed six months but less than one year of service.
The employer shall determine the dates of the annual leave, taking into consideration the worker's interests and the operational requirements. The worker may carry forward up to half of their annual leave entitlement to the following year. The employer shall pay the worker their full wage for the period of annual leave before the worker commences their leave.

Article 12:
A worker shall be entitled to sick leave of up to ninety days per year, calculated as follows:
(a) The first fifteen days: full pay.
(b) The next thirty days: half pay.
(c) The subsequent forty-five days: without pay.
The worker shall provide a medical certificate from a licensed medical practitioner to justify the sick leave. A worker shall not be entitled to sick leave during the probation period. The employer may terminate the employment of a worker who exhausts their sick leave and is unable to resume work, subject to the payment of all end-of-service benefits.

Article 13:
A female worker shall be entitled to maternity leave of sixty days, distributed as follows:
(a) Forty-five days with full pay.
(b) Fifteen days with half pay.
If the female worker gives birth to a sick child or a child with special needs requiring constant care, as certified by a medical authority, she shall be entitled to an additional thirty days of fully paid leave following the expiry of the maternity leave, which may be extended for a further thirty days without pay.

Article 14:
A male worker shall be entitled to paternity leave of five working days, to be taken within six months of the birth of the child. The leave may be taken consecutively or intermittently within the specified period.

Article 15:
A worker shall be entitled to the following special leave with full pay:
(a) Bereavement leave: Five working days upon the death of a spouse, and three working days upon the death of a parent, child, sibling, grandchild, or grandparent.
(b) Hajj leave: Up to thirty days once during the period of service, without pay, for performing the Hajj pilgrimage, provided the worker has completed one year of continuous service.
(c) Study leave: Ten working days per year for workers enrolled in an accredited educational institution within the State, provided the worker has completed two years of continuous service.

CHAPTER 4: Wages and Payment

Article 16:
The wage shall be paid in the currency of the United Arab Emirates (AED) through the Wages Protection System (WPS) approved by the Central Bank of the UAE. The employer shall pay the wage on the agreed-upon date, which shall not exceed fourteen days from the end of the pay period. If the employer fails to pay the wage on the due date without a legitimate reason, the Ministry may impose administrative penalties and order the immediate payment of the outstanding wages.

Article 17:
The employer shall not deduct more than the following from the worker's wage:
(a) Repayment of advances or loans, not exceeding ten percent of the monthly wage.
(b) Contributions to end-of-service savings schemes or pension funds as required by law.
(c) Deductions ordered by a court of competent jurisdiction, not exceeding twenty-five percent of the monthly wage.
(d) Deductions for damages caused by the worker to the employer's property, provided the amount does not exceed five days' wages per month and the worker has been given the opportunity to be heard.
In no case shall the total deductions exceed fifty percent of the worker's monthly wage.

Article 18:
If the worker works on a piece-rate, commission, or production basis, the employer shall ensure that the worker earns at least the minimum wage determined by the Council of Ministers. The employer shall keep records of all wages paid, deductions made, and overtime hours worked, and shall make such records available for inspection by the Ministry.

CHAPTER 5: Termination of Employment

Article 19:
The employment contract may be terminated in the following circumstances:
(a) Expiry of the contract term, unless renewed.
(b) Written agreement of both parties.
(c) Death of the worker, or permanent total disability preventing the worker from performing their work.
(d) A court judgment of imprisonment of the worker for a period exceeding three months.
(e) Closure of the establishment permanently, in accordance with applicable legislation.
(f) Termination by either party in accordance with the provisions of this Law.

Article 20:
Either party may terminate the employment contract for a legitimate reason by giving the other party written notice of not less than thirty days if the worker has served for less than five years, and not less than sixty days if the worker has served for five years or more. The notice period shall commence from the date on which the notice is served. During the notice period, the worker shall continue to work and the employer shall continue to pay the worker's full wage.

Article 21:
The employer may terminate the worker's employment without notice or end-of-service gratuity in the following cases:
(a) The worker assumes a false identity or submits forged documents.
(b) The worker commits an act resulting in substantial financial loss to the employer, provided the employer notifies the Ministry within seven working days of becoming aware of the incident.
(c) The worker violates safety instructions that endanger the life of the worker or others, provided such instructions are displayed in writing at a conspicuous place.
(d) The worker fails to perform basic duties despite receiving written warnings.
(e) The worker discloses confidential information of the employer.
(f) The worker is found in a state of intoxication or under the influence of narcotic substances during working hours.
(g) The worker assaults the employer, manager, or colleagues during work.
(h) The worker is absent without a legitimate reason for more than twenty intermittent days or seven consecutive days in one year.

Article 22:
The worker may leave work without notice while retaining all legal rights in the following cases:
(a) The employer fails to fulfill contractual or legal obligations towards the worker.
(b) The employer or their representative assaults or harasses the worker.
(c) There is a serious danger to the worker's health or safety in the workplace that the employer was aware of but failed to address.
(d) The employer assigns the worker to work that is substantially different from the work agreed upon in the contract without the worker's consent.

CHAPTER 6: End of Service Gratuity

Article 23:
A worker who has completed one or more years of continuous service shall be entitled to an end-of-service gratuity upon termination of employment. The gratuity shall be calculated as follows:
(a) Twenty-one days' basic wage for each year of the first five years of service.
(b) Thirty days' basic wage for each additional year of service beyond five years.
The total gratuity shall not exceed the equivalent of two years' basic wage. Days of absence from work without pay shall not be included in the calculation of the period of service.

Article 24:
If the worker's service is less than one year, no gratuity shall be payable. The gratuity shall be calculated based on the last basic wage the worker was receiving at the date of termination. If the worker is employed on a daily or weekly wage basis, the daily wage shall be calculated by dividing the total wages received during the last six months by the number of working days during that period.

Article 25:
A worker who is terminated for any of the reasons specified in Article 21 shall not be entitled to end-of-service gratuity. In all other cases of termination, including resignation by the worker after completing one year of service, the worker shall be entitled to the full end-of-service gratuity as calculated under Article 23.

CHAPTER 7: Workplace Safety and Worker Protections

Article 26:
The employer shall provide a safe and healthy working environment for workers and shall take all necessary measures to protect workers from occupational hazards and diseases. The employer shall:
(a) Provide appropriate personal protective equipment at no cost to the worker.
(b) Ensure proper ventilation, lighting, and sanitation in the workplace.
(c) Display safety instructions and emergency procedures in a language understood by the workers.
(d) Provide first aid facilities and trained first aid personnel.
(e) Report any work-related injury or occupational disease to the competent authority within forty-eight hours of its occurrence.

Article 27:
The employer shall provide adequate accommodation for workers if accommodation is a condition of the employment contract. The accommodation shall meet the standards prescribed by the competent authority in terms of space per worker, ventilation, sanitation, and safety. The employer shall bear the cost of the accommodation and shall not deduct it from the worker's wage unless otherwise agreed in the contract.

Article 28:
A worker who sustains an injury or contracts an occupational disease arising out of and in the course of employment shall be entitled to:
(a) Medical treatment and hospitalization at the employer's expense until recovery or stabilization.
(b) Full wage during the period of treatment, for a maximum of six months.
(c) Compensation for permanent partial or total disability, calculated as a percentage of the compensation for total disability in proportion to the degree of disability.
(d) If the injury results in death, the worker's family shall be entitled to compensation equivalent to twenty-four months of the worker's basic wage, with a minimum of AED 18000 and a maximum of AED 200000.

CHAPTER 8: Anti-Discrimination, Harassment, and Equal Opportunity

Article 29:
Discrimination in employment on the basis of race, color, sex, religion, national origin, ethnic origin, or disability is prohibited. The employer shall not discriminate between workers performing the same type of work in terms of wages, benefits, or conditions of employment. The term "discrimination" includes any distinction, exclusion, or preference that has the effect of nullifying or impairing equality of opportunity or treatment in employment.

Article 30:
Sexual harassment, bullying, and any form of verbal, physical, or psychological violence against workers in the workplace is strictly prohibited. The employer shall establish clear policies and procedures for the prevention of and response to harassment in the workplace. Workers who report harassment in good faith shall be protected from retaliation.

Article 31:
Female workers shall be entitled to equal remuneration for work of equal value. An employer shall not terminate a female worker or give her notice of termination during pregnancy or maternity leave. Any termination during such period shall be void and the employer shall be required to reinstate the worker or pay compensation.

CHAPTER 9: Dispute Resolution

Article 32:
Labour disputes shall be resolved through the following procedures:
(a) The worker or employer shall first file a complaint with the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation, which shall attempt to settle the dispute amicably within fourteen days.
(b) If the dispute is not settled amicably, the Ministry shall refer the matter to the competent Labour Court.
(c) Labour Court proceedings shall be expedited and shall be exempt from judicial fees for claims not exceeding AED 100000.
(d) The worker may file a complaint within one year of the date on which the disputed right became due.

Article 33:
The employer shall not terminate or threaten to terminate the worker for filing a complaint or participating in proceedings before the Ministry or the Labour Court. Any such termination shall be deemed arbitrary and the worker shall be entitled to compensation not exceeding three months' wage in addition to any other rights.

PART TWO: SUPPLEMENTARY AND TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

Article 34:
The employer shall maintain complete records of all workers, including personal details, employment dates, wages, leave records, and any disciplinary actions. Such records shall be maintained for a period of not less than two years after the termination of the worker's employment and shall be made available for inspection by the Ministry.

Article 35:
Employment contracts and arrangements that conflict with the provisions of this Law shall be amended to comply with this Law within one year of its effective date. Any clause in an employment contract that deprives the worker of rights guaranteed under this Law shall be void.

Article 36:
The employer shall not require the worker to surrender their passport, identity documents, or personal documents. Any confiscation of such documents by the employer shall constitute a criminal offence punishable by a fine not less than AED 10000 for each affected worker.

Article 37:
Workers performing domestic work shall be subject to the provisions of this Law to the extent that they do not conflict with the special regulations governing domestic workers. The Ministry shall issue specific regulations for domestic workers addressing working hours, rest periods, accommodation, and repatriation.

Article 38:
Foreign workers shall be entitled to repatriation to their home country at the expense of the employer upon termination of employment, unless the worker has moved to a new employer within the State. If the worker's employment is terminated due to the worker's fault under Article 21, the employer's obligation to pay repatriation costs shall remain.

Article 39:
The employer shall not employ workers without a valid work permit issued by the Ministry. Violation of this provision shall subject the employer to a fine of not less than AED 50000 for each illegally employed worker.

Article 40:
Juvenile workers under the age of eighteen shall not be employed in hazardous or arduous work as determined by the Ministry. Juvenile workers shall not work more than six hours per day, with at least one hour break included. Juvenile workers shall not be required to work overtime or between 7:00 PM and 7:00 AM.

Article 41:
The employer shall provide worker orientation programs upon commencement of employment, covering the worker's rights and obligations under this Law, safety procedures, and the grievance mechanism.

Article 42:
Non-competition clauses in employment contracts shall be valid only if they are limited in scope, time, and geography. Such clauses shall not exceed two years from the date of termination and shall not unreasonably restrict the worker's ability to earn a livelihood.

Article 43:
The employer shall grant the worker a certificate of service upon termination of employment, free of charge, specifying the period of employment, the nature of work performed, and the last wage received. The employer shall not include any information in the certificate that may prejudice the worker's ability to find new employment.

Article 44:
Any provision of Federal Law No. 8 of 1980 Concerning the Regulation of Labour Relations, and any other provision that contradicts or conflicts with this Law, shall be repealed. Regulations and decisions implementing repealed provisions shall remain in force until new implementing regulations are issued.

Article 45:
This Law shall be published in the Official Gazette and shall come into force on February 2, 2022.
