UAE PERSONAL STATUS LAW
Federal Law No. 28 of 2005
On Personal Status

================================================================================

PART ONE: MARRIAGE

CHAPTER 1: Engagement and Marriage Contract

Article 1:
This Law governs matters of personal status, including marriage, divorce, custody, inheritance, maintenance, and guardianship. The provisions of this Law shall apply to UAE nationals. Non-Muslim expatriates may choose to be governed by this Law or by their personal laws, subject to the provisions of the relevant legislation.

Article 2:
Marriage is a contract between a man and a woman that is legally sanctioned, the purpose of which is the establishment of a family based on care and affection. The marriage contract requires:
(a) Offer and acceptance expressed in clear and unequivocal terms.
(b) The consent of both parties, free from coercion or fraud.
(c) The presence of two witnesses.
(d) The specification of the dowry (mahr), whether prompt or deferred.
(e) The guardian (wali) of the bride, where required.

Article 3:
The age of marriage capacity shall be eighteen Gregorian years for both males and females. The judge may, upon the request of the guardian and with the approval of the competent medical committee, authorize the marriage of a person who has reached the age of sixteen if it is determined that the marriage serves the best interest of the parties.

Article 4:
The dowry (mahr) is the property given by the husband to the wife as a condition of the marriage contract. The dowry may be prompt (paid at the time of marriage) or deferred (payable upon a specified event or upon divorce or death). If the contract does not specify the amount of the dowry, the wife shall be entitled to a dowry equivalent to that of women of similar social status (mahr al-mithl).

Article 5:
A marriage shall be registered with the competent court or authorized authority. An unregistered marriage is valid but the parties may not invoke it before the courts unless it is proven by the means prescribed by law. The registration of marriage is essential for the protection of the rights of both parties and their children.

Article 6:
The marriage contract may contain conditions stipulated by either party, provided they do not conflict with the purpose of marriage, public order, or the mandatory provisions of Sharia. If the husband stipulates a condition that benefits the wife or vice versa, and the condition is violated, the aggrieved party may seek rescission of the marriage before the court.

CHAPTER 2: Rights and Obligations of Spouses

Article 7:
The husband shall be obligated to:
(a) Pay the prompt dowry upon the conclusion of the marriage contract.
(b) Provide the wife with suitable accommodation commensurate with their means.
(c) Provide maintenance (nafaqa) for the wife, including food, clothing, medical care, and all necessities of life.
(d) Treat the wife with kindness and respect.
(e) Not prevent the wife from visiting her parents and relatives in accordance with custom.

Article 8:
The wife shall be obligated to:
(a) Reside in the marital home provided by the husband.
(b) Permit the husband to have lawful intercourse.
(c) Care for the household and children to the extent of her ability.
(d) Obey the husband in matters that are lawful and customary.
The wife shall not be required to work outside the home, and if she does so with the husband's consent, she shall retain her earnings.

Article 9:
The wife's right to maintenance (nafaqa) shall be established from the date of the valid marriage contract if she makes herself available to the husband or offers to do so. Maintenance shall include food, clothing, accommodation, medical care, and domestic service if the wife is accustomed to having domestic help. The amount of maintenance shall be determined by the judge taking into account the husband's financial means and the prevailing standards of living.

Article 10:
If the husband fails to provide maintenance for the wife, she may apply to the court for an order requiring the husband to pay maintenance. The court may order the husband to pay maintenance retroactively from the date on which maintenance was due. If the husband is absent or his whereabouts are unknown, the court may order maintenance to be paid from the husband's property.

PART TWO: DIVORCE

CHAPTER 3: Types of Divorce

Article 11:
Divorce (talaq) is the dissolution of the marriage bond. Divorce may be:
(a) Revocable (raj'i): The husband may revoke the divorce during the waiting period (iddah) without a new contract or dowry. This applies to the first and second divorce.
(b) Irrevocable minor (ba'in sughra): The marriage bond is dissolved and the husband may remarry the wife only with a new contract and a new dowry. This applies to divorce before consummation and divorce by khul'.
(c) Irrevocable major (ba'in kubra): The marriage bond is dissolved and the husband may not remarry the wife until she has been married to and divorced from another husband in a genuine marriage.

Article 12:
The husband may divorce his wife by pronouncement in the presence of the wife or by written notification. A divorce pronounced under coercion, intoxication, or extreme anger that deprives a person of their reason shall have no effect. The divorce shall be registered with the competent court. Three pronouncements of divorce in a single session shall be counted as one divorce.

Article 13:
Khul' is the dissolution of the marriage at the request of the wife in return for compensation paid to the husband, typically the return of the dowry or other consideration. The court shall attempt reconciliation between the parties before granting khul'. If reconciliation fails and the wife insists on khul', the court shall order the dissolution of the marriage.

Article 14:
Either spouse may apply to the court for judicial divorce on the following grounds:
(a) Harm or discord (darar) that makes the continuation of married life impossible.
(b) Failure of the husband to provide maintenance for the wife for a period exceeding three months.
(c) Absence of the husband for more than one year without a legitimate reason and without providing maintenance.
(d) Imprisonment of the husband for a period exceeding three years.
(e) Impotence or an incurable disease that prevents marital relations, provided the other spouse was unaware of the condition at the time of marriage.
(f) Desertion or refusal to fulfill marital obligations.

Article 15:
The waiting period (iddah) following divorce shall be:
(a) For a woman who menstruates: three complete menstrual cycles from the date of divorce.
(b) For a woman who does not menstruate: three lunar months from the date of divorce.
(c) For a pregnant woman: until delivery.
During the waiting period following a revocable divorce, the husband shall provide the wife with maintenance and accommodation.

CHAPTER 4: Effects of Divorce

Article 16:
Upon divorce, the wife shall be entitled to:
(a) The deferred dowry, if any.
(b) Maintenance during the waiting period.
(c) Compensation for arbitrary divorce (mut'ah), determined by the court taking into account the duration of the marriage, the husband's financial means, and the circumstances of the divorce. The mut'ah shall not exceed the equivalent of one year's maintenance.
(d) Any property or assets to which she is entitled under the marriage contract or by law.

PART THREE: CHILD CUSTODY AND GUARDIANSHIP

CHAPTER 5: Custody (Hadana)

Article 17:
Custody (hadana) is the care, upbringing, and protection of a child who is unable to care for themselves. The custodian shall provide the child with food, clothing, shelter, education, and healthcare. Custody is both a right and a duty of the custodian.

Article 18:
The mother shall have priority in the custody of the child, followed by the maternal grandmother, then the paternal grandmother, then the father. If none of these persons is available or fit for custody, the court shall determine the most suitable custodian from among the child's relatives, taking into account the best interest of the child.

Article 19:
Custody shall end when the male child reaches the age of eleven years and the female child reaches the age of thirteen years, unless the court determines that the best interest of the child requires the extension of custody. Upon reaching the age of majority, the child shall have the right to choose which parent they wish to live with.

Article 20:
The custodian must meet the following conditions:
(a) Be of sound mind and of good moral character.
(b) Be capable of raising and caring for the child.
(c) Be free from contagious or dangerous diseases that could harm the child.
(d) Not have been convicted of a crime of moral turpitude.
(e) If the custodian is a woman, she must not be married to a man who is not related to the child within the prohibited degrees of kinship, unless the court determines that the child's interest requires otherwise.

Article 21:
The non-custodial parent shall have the right to visit the child at times determined by the court. The court shall establish a visitation schedule that serves the best interest of the child, taking into account the age of the child and the circumstances of both parents. Neither parent may prevent the other from exercising visitation rights without a court order.

Article 22:
The custodial parent shall not travel with the child outside the State without the written consent of the other parent or a court order. If the custodial parent wishes to relocate with the child to another emirate within the State, the court shall determine whether the relocation serves the best interest of the child.

CHAPTER 6: Guardianship

Article 23:
Guardianship (wilaya) over the person and property of a minor shall belong to the father, then to the paternal grandfather, then to the person designated by the father in their will, then to any person appointed by the court. The guardian shall act in the best interest of the minor and shall not dispose of the minor's property except in a manner that preserves or increases its value.

Article 24:
The guardian shall not, without the permission of the court, sell or mortgage the immovable property of the minor, donate the minor's property, borrow on behalf of the minor, or invest the minor's funds in speculative ventures. The court may remove the guardian if it is established that the guardian has mismanaged the minor's affairs or acted contrary to the minor's best interest.

PART FOUR: INHERITANCE AND SUCCESSION

CHAPTER 7: Inheritance (Mirath)

Article 25:
Inheritance is the transfer of the deceased's estate to their heirs as determined by Islamic Sharia. The estate shall be distributed after payment of:
(a) Funeral expenses and burial costs.
(b) Debts owed by the deceased.
(c) Bequests (wasiyyah) not exceeding one-third of the net estate, to the extent they benefit non-heirs or are permitted by the heirs.

Article 26:
The heirs shall be classified as follows:
(a) Fard heirs: Those entitled to fixed shares as prescribed by the Quran and Sunnah, including the spouse, parents, daughters, and siblings in certain cases.
(b) Asaba heirs: Male relatives who receive the remainder of the estate after the fard shares have been distributed.
(c) Dhawu al-arham: Relatives not classified as fard or asaba heirs, who may inherit in the absence of other heirs.

Article 27:
The shares of the fard heirs shall be as follows:
(a) The surviving wife shall receive one-quarter of the estate if the deceased has no children, and one-eighth if the deceased has children.
(b) The surviving husband shall receive one-half of the estate if the deceased wife has no children, and one-quarter if the deceased wife has children.
(c) The father shall receive one-sixth of the estate if the deceased has male children, and one-sixth plus the remainder if the deceased has only female children.
(d) The mother shall receive one-sixth if the deceased has children or two or more siblings, and one-third if the deceased has no children and fewer than two siblings.
(e) The daughter shall receive one-half if she is the only daughter, and two-thirds if there are two or more daughters. If there are both sons and daughters, the male shall receive twice the share of the female.

Article 28:
A person shall be excluded from inheritance if:
(a) They intentionally caused the death of the deceased.
(b) There is a difference of religion between the heir and the deceased; a Muslim shall not inherit from a non-Muslim and vice versa, unless the non-Muslim heir is the spouse of a UAE national.
(c) There is a difference of domicile between the heir and the deceased in certain cases involving foreign nationals, subject to applicable international agreements.

PART FIVE: MAINTENANCE AND SUPPLEMENTARY PROVISIONS

CHAPTER 8: Maintenance (Nafaqa)

Article 29:
A person shall be obligated to provide maintenance for those whom they are legally required to maintain. Maintenance includes food, clothing, accommodation, medical care, education, and other necessities. The order of obligation for maintenance is:
(a) The husband for the wife.
(b) The father for minor children.
(c) Adult children for their parents in need.
(d) Other relatives as determined by the court.

Article 30:
The father shall be obligated to provide maintenance for his children until they are able to support themselves. For male children, maintenance shall continue until they complete their education or reach the age at which they are able to earn a living. For female children, maintenance shall continue until marriage. If a child is disabled or incapable of earning a living, the father's obligation to provide maintenance shall continue regardless of the child's age.

Article 31:
If the father is unable to provide maintenance for his children, the obligation shall pass to the mother if she has sufficient means, without prejudice to her right to recover the amount from the father when he becomes able to pay. If neither parent is able to provide maintenance, the obligation shall pass to the grandparents or other relatives as determined by the court.

CHAPTER 9: Supplementary and Final Provisions

Article 32:
The court shall attempt reconciliation in all personal status disputes before proceeding to trial. The court may appoint one or two arbitrators from the families of both parties to assist in the reconciliation process. If reconciliation is not possible, the court shall proceed to adjudicate the dispute.

Article 33:
Matters of personal status involving non-Muslim foreigners may be governed by the Civil Personal Status Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 41 of 2022) if the parties choose to be governed by that law. The Civil Personal Status Law provides for civil marriage, civil divorce, and equal inheritance rights, and applies to non-Muslim expatriates who opt in to its provisions.

Article 34:
All personal status judgments shall be enforceable upon the parties. Appeals may be filed within thirty days of the date of notification of the judgment. The Court of Appeal shall have jurisdiction to review the facts and the law.

Article 35:
This Law shall be published in the Official Gazette and shall come into force three months after the date of its publication. The provisions of this Law shall be interpreted in accordance with the principles of Islamic Sharia and the purposes of family protection, the best interest of children, and the stability of the family unit.
